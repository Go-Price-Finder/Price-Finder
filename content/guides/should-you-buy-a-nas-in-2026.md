# Should You Buy a NAS in 2026?

## The drives are the decision. The box is the cheap part.

Western Digital has already sold every hard drive it will make this year. Not
most of them. All of them — on firm purchase orders, to seven customers.

None of those customers is you.

That one fact tells you more about buying a NAS in 2026 than any spec sheet
will.

## "Sold out" isn't a figure of speech here

On Western Digital's Q2 2026 earnings call in February, chief executive Irving
Tan didn't dress it up: *"we're pretty much sold out for calendar 2026.
We have firm POs with our top seven customers."*

Then he went further. Long-term agreements are already signed with two of those
customers for **2027**, and one for **2028** — locking in volume and price
years ahead. Seagate is in the same position, its capacity reported as
committed into 2027.

The price effect is not subtle. Across Western Digital's HDD models, the
average price is up **46% since September 2025.**

## You are not the customer

Here's the number that reframes the whole decision. At Western Digital, **cloud
customers are 89% of revenue. Consumers are 5%.**

This shortage is AI infrastructure. Data centres buy storage in volumes that
make the retail market a rounding error — and they buy it on contracts that
lock supply years out.

Which is why "wait for prices to come down" is weaker advice than it sounds.
Waiting works when consumer demand softens and sellers blink. Consumer demand
is 5% of this picture. There's no lever to wait for.

## Flash won't rescue you either

Solid state is caught in the same squeeze. NAND contract prices climbed roughly
60% in a single quarter in Q2 2026, with another 10–15% forecast for Q3 — same
AI buildout, same fabs.

And flash was never close to competitive at these capacities. Western Digital's
own comparison puts SSDs at **more than sixteen times the cost of equivalent
hard drives.**

A small SSD for cache or boot still makes sense. Flash for bulk storage is a
different budget, not a clever workaround.

## The old advice is now the wrong advice

**Your purchase has split in two.** Enclosures are ordinary consumer
electronics — competitive, seasonally discounted, behaving themselves. Drives
are a rationed commodity. Every NAS guide written before 2026 treats them as
one purchase, and that's precisely what leads you astray.

**"Buy bigger than you need" has become expensive.** It was sound advice for years, back when
storage got cheaper every time you looked. But at 46% in under twelve months,
you'd be paying a scarcity premium for terabytes you won't touch until 2029.

## Where you stand, and what to do

**You need storage now.** Buy the enclosure today — it isn't under pressure.
Then size the drives to the next 12–18 months, not the next five years.

**You're choosing between capacities.** Check price per terabyte on the day
you buy — not the week before. The old rule that mid-range capacities are the
sweet spot has stopped holding. These days the answer moves week to week.

**You're tempted to half-fill the bays.** Fair hedge — but check the exit
first. Growing an array later isn't always growing. Depending on your RAID
level and your vendor's tools, it can mean a long rebuild, or restoring from
backup instead. Find out what *your* model does before you buy two drives
rather than four.

**A drive in your array is failing.** Replace it today. This one isn't really
about price at all — it's a data decision wearing a price decision's clothes,
and losing the array will cost you far more than any premium you pay this
month.

**You're eyeing recertified enterprise drives.** A genuinely good option this
year, with genuine strings attached. They're cheaper and plentiful. They also
arrive with unknown power-on hours unless the seller documents them, shorter
warranties, and no history you can verify. Buy from someone who publishes SMART
data. Treat the warranty term as the actual product. And never let a
recertified drive hold your only copy of anything.

## What we can't tell you

Nobody knows when this turns. The figures above are reported statements and
quarterly forecasts, not promises, and this market has surprised people in both
directions before.

What's solid: four quarters of direction, and supply locked up well into 2027.
What isn't: the month it reverses.

And if you don't actually need a NAS, feel free to ignore all of it. This
guide is for someone who has already decided to buy, and is only working out
*when*.

## The habit that beats a forecast

Timing questions are hard for a boring reason: almost nobody can see what a
product cost last month. One price on one day tells you nothing about whether
it's a good price — which is exactly how a raised list price with a discount
stapled to it passes for a bargain.

So here's the thing worth holding on to. You don't need to predict this

Which takes about ten seconds: **write down what a drive costs when you start
looking, then check it again before you buy.**

If it's gone up, the market has answered your question.
